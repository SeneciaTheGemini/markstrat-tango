01 / Letter to shareholders

Same start line. Different finish.

To our shareholders,

Every firm in our industry began with the same two Sonites brands, the same $38.3M in revenue and the same share price. Nine periods later, Tango is the second most valuable firm of six, with five brands across two markets and $451M in cumulative pre-tax earnings. Every dollar we put into marketing, research and R&D returned $3.16 in pre-tax earnings.

Our approach: put every brand where a real segment wants it, and invest before growth stalls. When Sonites flattened, we spent $10.2M on R&D and took TIDE into Vodites, aimed at Followers, the segment forecast to double next period.

We did this as a team of two, Josh Butler and me, against teams of three and four, while still learning how the simulation worked. Some of its mechanics are still a puzzle to us.

Senecia Smith

11 / What I learned

We matched the industry when we should have matched the leader.

01

Protect share before ROI

Cutting spend in Period 6 looked efficient, but it gave N the opening to take our customers. Share lost to a rival is expensive to win back.

02

Debt can be a strategy

We avoided a loan to fund Vodites. N borrowed, reached Vodites a period ahead of us and outspent us in Sonites.

03

Let segment data lead

Where we did invest, it worked: brands placed on segment ideal points returned $3.60 or more per marketing dollar.

04

Learn the system while you play it

With two people against teams of three and four, we were still decoding the simulation. We leaned on market research and planned extra production as a buffer.
